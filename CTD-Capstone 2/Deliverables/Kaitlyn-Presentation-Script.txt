SLIDE 2 · MEET THE TEAM (my line, about 7 seconds)
======================================================================

I'm Kaitlyn. I'm the project lead, and I handle the controls and code:
planning and coordination, the Arduino circuits, helping build the
TouchDesigner logic and visuals, and making it all work together.


======================================================================
SLIDE 3 · THE ELEVATOR PITCH (about 20 seconds)
======================================================================

Here's MetamorphUs in one breath. It's an interactive installation where
strangers use physical controllers, like a sun crank or a water pump, to
bring a dying digital ecosystem back to life.

No one can do it alone. Only when the group keeps sunlight, water, wind,
heat and nutrients in balance together does the whole world transform
into a glowing butterfly.


======================================================================
SLIDE 9 · PRECEDENT 3: HOT SWAP (about 40 seconds)
(The new slide right after the old alt.ctrl.GDC slide)
======================================================================

Our third precedent is HOT SWAP: All Hands On Deck, from alt.ctrl.GDC, a
showcase of games with custom physical controllers. Two players fly a
ship using five magnetic, 3D-printed inputs but only four slots, so one
piece is always out of play and they have to hand pieces to each other
mid-game.

In playtests, some people set pieces out for their partner, and others
reached across to grab what they needed, which is the kind of contact we
want between strangers. It's built with an Arduino Micro, plus pogo pins
and custom circuit boards for each piece.

The game starts calm, with about one enemy every 10 seconds, so players
learn the controls and each other first. One of the makers says his favorite
moment is when a perfectly coordinated team falls into chaos once the
ship catches fire. For us, that means our surprise events should come
after a group has found its rhythm.

Where it stops short is scale: it's a two-player game. Our challenge is
four to five strangers.


======================================================================
SLIDE 11 · TECHNICAL TEST: ARDUINO BUTTONS (about 20 seconds)
======================================================================

My first technical test was really simple. It only tested two things:
can an Arduino tell when two people press at the same time, and does a
small light help people figure that out on their own?

Two buttons, a red light for "half right," and a green light for success.
Successful presses landed 16 to 46 milliseconds apart, and our peer Mia
figured out she had to press both together within seconds, with no
instructions except "light the green LED."

That was the first iteration. I'll show you where I took it next in a
second.

   [Optional, if you have time: "We also learned to check our wiring.
   At first both buttons read as always pressed until we turned them."]


======================================================================
SLIDE 13 · TECHNICAL TEST: GLOW TEST (about 45 seconds)
======================================================================

For the second iteration, I took that same two-button circuit and
connected it to a glowing digital world. I built the same small scene
twice, once in p5.js and once in TouchDesigner.

Holding a button raises an element, like sunlight or water, and letting
go drains it, so two people have to keep both in the sweet spot. Hold the
balance for six seconds, and the lights gather into a butterfly. Both
versions worked live with the same circuit and the same Arduino code.

I really liked working in TouchDesigner. Its node-based interface makes
it easy to see how everything connects, and it has a huge range of
operators that each do something different, plus built-in effects and
lots of ways to connect to microcontrollers and other tools. That makes
it a strong fit for MetamorphUs, so it's our leading choice. I'm going to
keep learning it and testing ideas in it, which will help us a lot next
semester.


======================================================================
SLIDE 14 · TECHNOLOGY RESEARCH (about 15 seconds)
======================================================================

Our research covers every layer, from a person's hands to the glowing
world. For sensing, we're starting with simple inputs like knobs,
buttons and cranks. An Arduino sends those to our software, and after
our glow test, TouchDesigner is our leading choice. For display, we'll
start with a large screen and explore projection, lights and sound.


======================================================================
SLIDE 17 · DOMAINS OF INTEREST (about 15 seconds)
======================================================================

MetamorphUs sits where social connection, collective play and nature
meet interactive art. On the tools side, it's physical computing meeting
creative coding: Arduino and sensors, TouchDesigner for the world,
fabrication for the controllers, and a big screen with light and sound.
