# CTD Senior Capstone: Where We Started and Where We Are

*CU Boulder Creative Technology & Design, 2026–2027 · Summary as of November 1, 2026*

This is the story of the project so far, from the first goal to the four concepts we're looking at now. Every doc mentioned here is linked so you can open the full version. Nothing below is a final decision unless it says so. The repo's decision log still has no confirmed concept, audience, medium, or technology.

---

## 1. Where we started: the goal

We started with an open goal: **make an immersive, interactive experience that carries real meaning or emotion.** We were especially drawn to pieces that use something personal about the visitor, like their **breath, pulse, heartbeat, movement, face, or body heat**, so the piece responds to how that person actually is right now.

That goal got sharper as we went:

- **Object-scale, not whole rooms.** We cared more about a mirror, a wall, a table, or a soft object that makes people feel or react than about a full immersive room.
- **It should make people feel something.** We kept coming back to surprise, comfort, connection, reflection, focus, "seeing your own nervous system," and the sense of people who came before you.
- **Installations and artworks, not products.** When Al Samuelian asked for a "why it matters" line for each concept (so it isn't tech for the sake of tech), we framed each concept around the meaning it carries.

An earlier clothes-drying-rack direction was explored before all this and is not active.

## 4. Where we are now: the concepts we're looking at

From all of that, four directions got their own deep "ways to build it" docs on Oct 28: **Flip Book Wall, Synchrony Reward, a combination of the two, and Echo of the Last Person.** Each doc lays out options tagged *Established / Adapted / Speculative*, deliberately unranked, with sensing, materials, skills, rough cost, and optional experiment seeds.

### Flip Book Wall
**[Flip Book Wall: Ways to Build It](https://claude.ai/artifact/2PNxAPqvCFH8eh7ziC3XsM)**

A split-flap wall whose picture grows with the number of people in front of it: **a leaf, then a branch, then a tree, then a forest.** It's about how what we make together is bigger than what one person can make.

- **Mechanisms:** split-flap (a motor per module, a shared axle per row, or one motor for the whole wall), prisms, roller blinds, flip-discs, Rozin-style tilting tiles, a crowd-weight floor, cranks, projection on blank flaps, a walk-in forest of columns, and more.
- **Key insight:** every flap steps through the same few images together, so shared-motor builds can cut cost a lot.
- Includes rough parts cost by grid size (4×4 up to 20×11).

### Synchrony Reward
**[Synchrony Reward: Ways to Build It](https://claude.ai/artifact/QWoE1fPBE8HX9RYY5bXgEc)**

A mirror or surface that only clears when **two people breathe or move in sync.** It's about connection and attunement. This is the concept closest to our original "use something personal like breath or pulse" goal.

- **What gets synced:** breath, heartbeat, sway or rocking, mirrored gestures, steps, humming, stillness.
- **Sensing:** stretch bands, mmWave radar, webcam chest motion, depth or thermal cameras, mic, load cells, pulse sensors, phones.
- **Surfaces:** screen, LED, or projection; smart film, fog, water, or polarizers; tilting tiles or fins; rocking chairs, a booth, shared VR.
- **Key insight:** the surface only has to show one 0–1 sync score, so the sensing and sync code can be built first and reused on any surface.
- Includes notes on health-signal, hygiene, and data care.

### Flip Wall + Synchrony combined
**[Flip Wall + Synchrony: Where They Overlap](https://claude.ai/artifact/PLJKaQjEszEEP68HDWWAEW)**

One piece where **group size picks the picture and shared breathing makes the flaps settle** out of a flutter into the image.

- Eight possible rules for joining them, such as "count picks, sync settles," "sync turns the page," "only in-step people count," and "the forest grows all Expo."
- **Key insight:** the flip wall comes down to one number (which picture) and synchrony to another (how in-sync people are), so merging them is mostly a matter of choosing the rule that links the two. A flutter effect needs a motor per flap. "Unlock then flip" keeps the cheaper shared-motor builds possible.
- **Hardest new part:** reading breathing rhythms for many people at once.

### Echo of the Last Person
**[Echo of the Last Person: Ways to Build It](https://claude.ai/artifact/UgYySbCfoRdHMgq2rFLaDs)**

A mirror that shows **a softened echo of the previous visitor** beside you, so you share the space with someone who came before you.

- **Four jobs:** capture, soften, hand off, show.
- **Surfaces:** two-way glass, Pepper's ghost, scrim, shadow, or LED; the Rozin family (wooden tiles, PomPom fur, flip-disc, pin relief); a servo puppet; a floor echo; sound only; AR or VR. There are also no-computer versions like a phosphorescent shadow wall or breath on glass.
- **Key insight:** the echo is meant to be soft, so low-resolution mechanical surfaces are a strength. Storing only a pose skeleton is tiny and faceless, and it can drive any surface, which also helps with privacy.
- **Precedents:** Rozin's Wooden Mirror and PomPom Mirror, Snibbe's Deep Walls, Lozano-Hemmer's Under Scan, Exploratorium Shadow Box.
