**What is your proposed project / domain & problem you are designing around?**

MetamorphUs is an interactive installation in the domains of social connection, collective play and environmental interdependence. Strangers in public spaces stand side by side but rarely interact; the U.S. Surgeon General reported in 2023 that about half of U.S. adults felt measurable loneliness even before COVID-19. We are designing an experience where cooperating with strangers is the only way to succeed: 4 to 5 people each control one element of a dying digital ecosystem with a physical controller, and only by keeping every element in balance together can they bring the world to life and transform it into a glowing butterfly.

## Precedents List by Pattern

### Pattern 01: Living Digital Ecosystems
Digital worlds that grow, bloom and fade in response to people.

**#1. Story of the Forest (teamLab)**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://www.teamlab.art/w/story-of-the-forest/nms/
Brief Description: The National Museum of Singapore’s Glass Rotunda, a drum-shaped space with a domed ceiling 15 meters high, becomes a digital forest of flowers, plants and animals native to Singapore and its region.
Reflection: A digital ecosystem can feel alive and worth caring for. Glow, scale and slow change create wonder, and it became the visual model for our balanced forest. Visitors experience it side by side, without needing each other, which is what we add.

**#2. Animals of Flowers, Symbiotic Lives (teamLab)**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://www.teamlab.art/w/symbiotic/
Brief Description: Seasonal flowers bloom and die in an endless cycle, forming the shapes of animals. When people touch an animal its petals scatter; touch it too much and all its flowers scatter.
Reflection: A creature made of many small living pieces feels magical, and knowing it can fall apart makes it precious. This inspired our butterfly made of the whole ecosystem, and the idea that it scatters when the group breaks apart.

**#3. Graffiti Nature (teamLab)**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://www.teamlab.art/w/graffitinature/aiz/
Brief Description: Visitors color a creature on paper and see it come to life in one shared ecosystem. Creatures that eat multiply; creatures that don’t eat, or get eaten, die and disappear.
Reflection: A few simple rules (eat, multiply, fade) make a world feel alive, and seeing your own contribution creates investment. Every contribution is individual, though; nobody needs anyone else.

### Pattern 02: Strangers Acting Together in Public
Works where people who don’t know each other end up coordinating.

**#1. 21 Balançoires (21 Swings), Daily tous les jours**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://dailytouslesjours.com/en/work/21-swings
Brief Description: Public musical swings in Montreal’s Quartier des spectacles, returning every spring since 2011. Each swing plays an instrument, and the higher you swing, the higher the note. Swinging in unison unlocks secret, more complex melodies.
Reflection: The closest match to our goal: strangers in a public space, a familiar action, and a hidden reward only cooperation unlocks. You still get notes swinging alone, so teamwork is a bonus; in MetamorphUs the world can’t be saved alone.

**#2. Cinematrix crowd Pong (Loren and Rachel Carpenter, SIGGRAPH 1991)**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://www.amygoodchild.com/blog/collaborative-control-games
Brief Description: Audience members held paddles with a green side and a red side; a camera counted the colors, and each half of the room steered one Pong paddle together.
Reflection: A room of strangers can coordinate with no instructions; Carpenter described it as “a kind of a subconscious consensus.” Everyone stays anonymous, though, so nobody meets anyone.

**#3. Pulse Room (Rafael Lozano-Hemmer, 2006)**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://www.lozano-hemmer.com/pulse_room.php
Brief Description: A visitor holds a sensor and the nearest of 100 to 300 hanging bulbs flashes at their heart rate; each new pulse pushes earlier ones down the grid, so the room shows recent visitors’ heartbeats.
Reflection: One simple sensor plus light makes strangers’ presence visible and shared. People take turns one at a time, so there is no cooperation.

### Pattern 03: Custom Controllers That Force Teamwork
Games from alt.ctrl.GDC, a showcase of games with custom physical controllers.

**#1. Cosmic Crew**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://gamedeveloper.com/marketing/cosmic-crew-different-co-op-controllers-keep-a-starship-intact
Brief Description: A Gunner aims a physical turret while a Mechanic hammers metal plates and loads ammo. Only the Gunner sees enemy weaknesses; ships only take damage from matching ammo. One Arduino Uno runs both controllers.
Reflection: Splitting information between stations makes talking necessary, and it was built with the same Arduino tech we use.

**#2. Two By 4 Racing**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/alt-ctrl-gdc-2023/how-constraints-fueled-two-by-4-racing-s-co-op-steering-wheel-controllers
Brief Description: Two players share one car: the front player steers with a bike-tire wheel and the rear player controls speed with an engine crank. An Arduino reads the buttons; rotary encoders read the wheels.
Reflection: A crank is a natural keep-moving control, like a sun crank. Both games are built for two people who came together, while we are designing for 4 to 5 strangers.

**#3. HOT SWAP: All Hands On Deck**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/alt-ctrl-gdc-showcase-i-hot-swap-all-hands-on-deck-i-
Brief Description: Five 3D-printed inputs share four slots on a ship controller, so two players must trade pieces mid-game. Built with an Arduino Micro and a JavaScript game.
Reflection: Handing objects to each other creates contact between people. The source notes coordination often breaks down when the ship catches fire, so chaos can swamp teamwork.

**#4. Vaccination**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://gamedeveloper.com/marketing/alt-ctrl-gdc-showcase-i-vaccination-i-
Brief Description: A Scanner finds bacteria on a screen and tells the Injector, who treats a physical patient. Built with a Makey Makey and a 3D-printed injector.
Reflection: An unsuccessful first version: the single-player prototype overwhelmed new players. Split between two people, it became easier to learn and more fun. Evidence that dividing a task between people helps.

### Pattern 04: Co-op Games Built on Talking
Screen games where communication is the whole game.

**#1. Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://www.keeptalkinggame.com/
Brief Description: One player sees a bomb but has no manual; the Experts have the manual but can’t see the bomb. They must describe and relay instructions before time runs out.
Reflection: Splitting what people can see makes talking the core mechanic, an idea for hints that show one station what another needs.

**#2. Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://www.loversinadangerousspacetime.com/
Brief Description: Two to four people crew one shared ship, each handling a station (weapons, shields, engines) and switching as needed.
Reflection: Stations around one shared vehicle mirrors our controllers around one shared world: “All players fill important roles at all times, and nobody gets left behind.”

**#3. Spaceteam**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://sleepingbeastgames.com/projects/spaceteam/
Brief Description: Described by its makers as “a cooperative shouting game” for phones and tablets.
Reflection: Loud group energy draws onlookers, the crowd-drawing effect our professor pointed to with Pearl Street buskers. Players usually play with friends on their own devices, not strangers.

### Pattern 05: Touch and the Body as Input
Low-cost physical inputs that invite people to play with their hands and bodies.

**#1. Wobble Garden (Robin Baumgarten)**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://magazine.raspberrypi.com/articles/interactive-wobble-garden
Brief Description: Players touch and wobble a field of sensing springs lit by LED rings; the prototype has 48 springs and the larger Quantum Garden has 228.
Reflection: A spread-out field of tactile controls invites a group to play at once, and springy, wobbly inputs feel joyful.

**#2. Energy Stick human circuit (Steve Spangler Science)**
[Add image]
Link/Source: https://stevespangler.com/experiments/human-circuit/
Brief Description: A group holds hands in a circle while two people hold the ends of the stick; the body closes the circuit and it flashes and buzzes until someone lets go.
Reflection: A cheap, proven way to sense that everyone is physically connected. Some strangers won’t want to hold hands, so it would stay optional.

**What were your overall findings about the precedents you found? Include a brief summary of patterns.**

We grouped 15 precedents into five patterns: living digital ecosystems, strangers acting together in public, custom controllers that force teamwork, co-op games built on talking, and touch and the body as input. Immersive ecosystems like teamLab’s show that a digital world can feel alive and worth caring for, but visitors never need each other. Public works like 21 Balançoires show strangers will cooperate when the action is familiar and the reward is a secret only teamwork unlocks. Co-op controller games show that split roles at physical stations make people talk, and that this is buildable with Arduino. Almost no precedent combines all three: a living world, strangers, and cooperation that is required rather than optional. That gap is where MetamorphUs sits.

**How will you move forward with these findings? How will this affect your Capstone? What was surprising / unexpected?**

What surprised us: a single-player version can fail where a shared one succeeds. Vaccination’s solo prototype overwhelmed new players, and splitting it between two people made it easier and more fun. We were also surprised how little technology the most social works need: swings, paddles and springs. Moving forward, we will keep the technology simple and spend our effort on what the precedents show matters most: a familiar action for each element, a world worth caring for, and a reward that only cooperation unlocks. We will also design a rule so one person can’t play alone, since most precedents let people succeed solo.
