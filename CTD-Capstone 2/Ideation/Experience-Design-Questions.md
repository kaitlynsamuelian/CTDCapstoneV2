# Six open-ended questions for exploring experience-based concepts

**Status: exploratory framework — NOT a project decision, assignment, or form that must be completed.**

Use these as separate creative lenses to generate, vary, and compare ideas. A concept may start from a technology, emotional goal, interaction, audience, or environment; questions can remain unresolved until later. Avoid assuming the black box, mirror, or any other example is the selected direction.

| Question | Possibilities / inspiration (non-exhaustive) |
|---|---|
| **1. WHERE does the experience happen?** | Black box, computer, phone, tablet, VR headset, tabletop, physical installation, wearable, ordinary object, public space, enclosed booth, or somewhere unexpected |
| **2. HOW does someone interact with it?** | Touch, movement, gestures, voice, breathing, heartbeat, eye movement, facial expressions, choices, drawing, writing, physical objects, collaborating with another person, or simply observing |
| **3. WHAT does someone experience or feel?** | Wonder, awe, nostalgia, calm, excitement, curiosity, empathy, connection, belonging, discomfort, surprise, reflection, altered perception, or being transported somewhere else |
| **4. HOW does the experience respond or change?** | Lighting, transforming visuals, evolving sound, moving objects, response to physiological signals, branching narrative, emerging elements, adaptation to expressed states, or combining inputs from multiple people; it could also be intentionally passive |
| **5. WHO is the experience for?** | College students, stressed individuals, strangers, friends, couples, families, older adults, people experiencing loneliness, art audiences, Expo visitors, or another specific group |
| **6. WHAT is the journey / arc?** | Entry, discovery, transformation, collaboration, what the visitor leaves with; fixed or open-ended sequence; one-person, pair, or crowd; short or long; visitors may leave a trace for the next person |

## Some journey questions to explore later
- Does someone enter an unfamiliar place and gradually discover how it works?
- Does the experience transform as they interact with it?
- Do they enter in one emotional state and leave potentially feeling another?
- Is something about the participant gradually revealed?
- Do two strangers enter separately but influence one another?
- Can a visitor leave something that changes the experience for the next person?
- Can visitors enter/leave freely, or does it have a clear beginning and ending?

## How Claude should use this
- Offer surprising combinations or explore one lens at a time when prompted.
- Never treat any possibility as confirmed without explicit team agreement.
- Do **not** turn this into an obligatory six-field worksheet for every idea.
- Keep technical feasibility and Expo constraints in view when eventually narrowing, but don't use them to squash early creativity unless asked.
- Differentiate **format/medium**, **input**, **responsive mechanism**, **felt experience**, **audience**, and **experience arc**; they are related but not interchangeable.
